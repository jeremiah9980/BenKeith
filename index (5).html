Thursday evening, less than a mile from home

Three minutes.

That's how long it took for an ordinary drive home to become a burning car with our son trapped inside. It's also about how long it took for strangers to decide he was worth running toward.

What happened

Cooper never saw it coming. Neither did anyone else.

Cooper was driving back from donating plasma when he lost consciousness at the wheel. The car left the road, rolled, and caught fire. The safety systems that are supposed to protect you in a crash held him in place, and he couldn't get himself out.

Ben Keith was getting into his own car nearby when dirt, mud, and pieces of a vehicle rained down on him. He turned around and ran at the fire. He pulled Cooper out of the car and got him behind a dumpster, in case the car exploded. Kristen Snook and her husband, and others whose names we're still collecting, stayed with Cooper on the ground so he wasn't alone.

Jesica got there seven minutes after the crash alert hit her phone. Cooper spent that night and most of the next day in the ICU. When he came to, one of the first things he asked was whether anyone had gotten the names of the people who helped him.

"Those people saved my life." Cooper, the day after

Seven minutes. He was never alone for any of them.

The people

They thought they were stopping at an accident. To us they're the reason we still have him.

Pulled him out

Ben Keith

Got showered with debris while getting into his car, ran toward the fire, pulled Cooper from the wreck, and moved him behind a dumpster in case it blew. Didn't wait to see if anyone else would.

Stayed with him

Kristen Snook & her husband

Kept Cooper calm and covered on the ground, and made sure he was not alone for a single minute until his mom got there.

Everyone else

The ones we're still finding

People who called 911, called Jesica, held a light, waved traffic off, or just stood close. If that was you, or you know who it was, we want your name. Not for anything but to say it back to you.
